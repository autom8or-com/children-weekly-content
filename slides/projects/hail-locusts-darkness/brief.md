- teaching points:      # 8 diary cards, authored ONCE here → transcribed verbatim into diary-cards. teacher_note → speaker-notes pane.
    - point:        God is never late
      teacher_note: God set the hour in advance — "about this time tomorrow" (Exo 9:18). He is never early, never late; His timing always plays out in His children's favour.
      angela_line:  "God is never late. Things will play out in my favour."
      scripture:    Exodus 9:18
      pull_slide:   # (hail warning scene)
    - point:        Call on the name of the Lord and be saved
      teacher_note: Those who feared the Lord's word brought their servants and flocks inside and were saved from the hail (Exo 9:20-21). Calling on the Lord in trouble is what rescues.
      angela_line:  "Whoever calls the name of the Lord will be saved. Those who took their flocks inside were saved. If I ever call the name of the Lord in trouble, I will be saved."
      scripture:    Exodus 9:19-21
      pull_slide:   # (those who sheltered vs those who didn't)
    - point:        Be sincere when you say sorry
      teacher_note: Pharaoh said "I have sinned… the Lord is right" (Exo 9:27), but the moment the hail stopped he hardened his heart again (9:34-35). Calling yourself a sinner is not the same as being sorry.
      angela_line:  "Not everyone who calls himself a sinner is really sorry. Pharaoh called himself a sinner but was never sorry — soon he hardened his heart. I must be sincere when I ask the Lord for forgiveness."
      scripture:    Exodus 9:27-35
      pull_slide:   # (Pharaoh's false confession)
    - point:        Do not ignore godly counsel
      teacher_note: Pharaoh's own officials warned him — "do you not yet realize that Egypt is ruined?" (Exo 10:7) — and he would not listen. A child who will perish ignores godly counsel and instruction.
      angela_line:  "A child that will perish will ignore godly counsel and instruction. Pharaoh's officials warned him, but he would not listen."
      scripture:    Exodus 10:7
      pull_slide:   # (officials pleading with Pharaoh)
    - point:        God wants your TOTAL freedom
      teacher_note: Satan, like stubborn Pharaoh, does not like the total freedom of a child of God. Pharaoh offered a partial deal — "go, but only the men" (Exo 10:8-11). God's freedom is never partial.
      angela_line:  "Satan, like stubborn Pharaoh, does not like the total freedom of a child of God. He said to Moses, 'Go with only the men to worship God.'"
      scripture:    Exodus 10:8-11
      pull_slide:   # (Pharaoh's partial offer / Moses driven out)
    - point:        The one God speaks to is powerful
      teacher_note: Moses could declare "not a hut will remain" untouched by locusts because he spoke for God. A child who walks with God can face a bully and report it, trusting God to deliver.
      angela_line:  "One whom the Lord speaks to is very powerful. Moses said to Pharaoh, 'Not a hut will remain.' When you are bullied in class, you can face the bully and report it, trusting God to deliver you."
      scripture:    Exodus 10:5-6
      pull_slide:   # (Moses declaring the locust warning)
    - point:        Light in a dark world
      teacher_note: Thick darkness covered Egypt for three days, yet the Israelites had light where they lived (Exo 10:22-23). We live IN this dark world as light, but we are not OF it.
      angela_line:  "Goshen had light while the rest of Egypt was in darkness. I live in this world as light, but I am not of this world that is full of darkness."
      scripture:    Exodus 10:21-23
      pull_slide:   # (Goshen light vs Egypt darkness — the double-duty scene)
    - point:        God speaks to me as I need Him
      teacher_note: Cross-cutting — Scripture never records Moses climbing a mountain in this whole stretch; God spoke to him per need, mouth-to-mouth. His relationship with the Lord was never broken, and neither is ours.
      angela_line:  "God spoke to Moses mouth-to-mouth. He can speak to me too. His relationship with the Lord was never broken."
      scripture:    (cross-cutting — Exodus 9-10 pattern)
      pull_slide:   # (a Moses-hearing-God scene)

## 5. Story beats (ordered → illustrations + story slides)
# Derived backward from the §2 diary points — every beat exists to set up a teaching point.
### Plague 7 — Hail (Exo 9:13-35)
- Moses warns Pharaoh at dawn: "about this time tomorrow" the worst hailstorm falls — bring your servants and flocks inside          → pts 1, 2
- Those who feared the Lord's word shelter their people and animals; those who ignored it leave theirs in the open field           → pt 2
- Hail and fire crash down across Egypt, striking every field, tree and crop — thunder rolling                                       → (impact scene)
- Goshen stands clear and untouched — no hail where God's people live (distinction)
- Pharaoh's false confession: "I have sinned" — then the hail stops and his heart hardens again                                      → pt 3
### Plague 8 — Locusts (Exo 10:1-20)
- Moses declares the warning: locusts will cover the land and fill every house — "not a hut will remain" untouched                   → pt 6
- Pharaoh's officials plead with him: "do you not realize Egypt is ruined? Let the people go"                                        → pt 4
- Pharaoh's partial offer — "go, but only the men" — and Moses is driven from his presence                                          → pt 5
- The east wind brings a black swarm that covers the ground and eats every green thing the hail had left
### Plague 9 — Darkness (Exo 10:21-29)
- Moses stretches out his hand — thick darkness you can feel covers Egypt for three days
- Goshen glows warm with light while the rest of Egypt sits in darkness (the light-vs-darkness contrast — story AND diary pull)      → pt 7
- Pharaoh's last partial offer — "go, but leave your flocks" — Moses refuses; Pharaoh threatens "the day you see my face you will die"
### Pivot (Exo 11:1-10)
- NOT a story beat this week — foreshadowed in one line on the goodbye slide (the final plague is coming next week)
