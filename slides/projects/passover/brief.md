## 2. Lesson (the teaching spine)
- theme (one line):     The blood of the Lamb saves — and God's promises always come true
- memory verse:         "When I see the blood, I will pass over you." — Exodus 12:13 (NIV)
- bible text:           Exodus 12:1-13 (Passover instructions) + 12:14-20 (Feast of Unleavened Bread) + 12:21-36 (Moses relays · death of firstborn · Pharaoh's plea) + 12:37-51 (The Exodus · Passover law)    # → FOUR bible-text slides, full chapter
- objectives:           # → objectives slide ("children will…")
    - Retell the Passover story: the lamb, the blood, the destroyer, the departure
    - Explain what the blood on the door means
    - See God as a promise-keeper who delivers His people with a mighty hand
    - Choose to keep hearing and obeying God, no matter how others respond
- big questions:
    - Why did God tell the Israelites to put blood on their doors?
    - How did God keep His promise to deliver His people?
    - What does the Passover lamb remind us of today?
- angela study title:   **Mighty Hand, Tender Care**
- application:          "If you are saved, thank God for His tender care. The Israelites were saved because they obeyed. If the blood of Jesus has not washed away your sin, you are marked for death!" — the A-B-C rescue steps follow
- teaching points:      # 4 diary cards, authored ONCE here → transcribed verbatim into diary-cards. teacher_note → speaker-notes pane.
    - point:        Pray for those who despitefully use you
      teacher_note: The Israelites had been enslaved and mistreated for generations. God could have told them to take revenge, but instead He told them to ASK the Egyptians for their wages (Exo 12:35-36) — and the Egyptians gave freely. Blessing, not cursing, is the way of the Kingdom (Matt 5:44).
      angela_line:  "I should pray for those who despitefully use me. I certainly will because the children of Israel got the wages for their labor before departure."
      scripture:    Exodus 12:35-36; Matthew 5:44
      pull_slide:   # (Israelites receiving silver/gold from Egyptians)
    - point:        The blood of the Lamb must be on me
      teacher_note: The destroyer passed over every house marked with blood. The Passover lamb is a picture of Jesus — the Lamb of God whose blood covers us (1 Cor 5:7; 1 Pet 1:18-19). Without the blood, there is no covering.
      angela_line:  "The blood of Jesus must be on me as a sign that I am a child of God, else the messenger of death will kill me as he killed the Egyptians."
      scripture:    Exodus 12:13; 1 Corinthians 5:7
      pull_slide:   # (the marked door — blood on lintel, family safe inside)
    - point:        Whatever God promises, He fulfills
      teacher_note: God told Moses He would deliver Israel with a mighty hand (Exo 3:19-20; 6:1). On that very night, Pharaoh rose in the dark and begged Israel to leave. God's word never returns empty (Isa 55:11).
      angela_line:  "God promised Moses that he will deliver the Israelites by a powerful hand and he did. Whatever God promises me, He will fulfill. I believe!"
      scripture:    Exodus 12:31-33, 51
      pull_slide:   # (Pharaoh at midnight, summoning Moses)
    - point:        Keep hearing God, no matter what
      teacher_note: Moses heard God and kept believing even after Pharaoh refused him time and again. His relationship with the Lord was never broken. "All things are possible to him that believes" (Mark 9:23) — a child who keeps hearing God can face any Pharaoh.
      angela_line:  "I like to hear God like Moses heard and believed despite Pharaoh's past disobedience."
      scripture:    Exodus 12:1, 21; Mark 9:23
      pull_slide:   # (Moses hearing God — memory bubble scene)

## 5. Story beats (ordered → illustrations + story slides)
# Derived backward from the §2 diary points — every beat exists to set up a teaching point.
### Passover instructions (Exo 12:1-13)
- God tells Moses and Aaron the calendar — month 1, day 10 select a lamb, day 14 kill it at twilight            → (memory-bubble scene for pt 4)
- An Israelite family selects a blemish-free lamb on day 10 and brings it home                                 → (warm domestic scene)
- Day 14 evening — the family slaughters the lamb, brushes its blood on the lintel and doorposts              → pt 2
- The family eats the Passover meal inside — roasted lamb, unleavened bread, bitter herbs                       → (intimate indoor scene)
### The destroyer passes (Exo 12:21-32)
- Midnight — the destroyer sweeps across Egypt; houses with the blood are passed over                          → pt 2 (impact scene, wordless)
- Death of the firstborn across the land (gentle — sleeping, peaceful, no graphic)                             → (impact scene, wordless)
- Pharaoh rises in the dark, summons Moses and Aaron: "Rise up, go, you and all Israel!"                       → pt 3
- The Egyptians urge the Israelites to leave at once — "otherwise we'll all die!"                              → (urgency scene)
### Wages & departure (Exo 12:33-42)
- The Israelites ask the Egyptians for silver, gold and clothing; the Egyptians give freely (the wages!)      → pt 1
- The Israelites leave Rameses — men, women, children, flocks and herds — heading for Succoth                  → (the big march-out)
- A Passover generation — "keep this day as a permanent law" — to this day the Israelites remember              → (closing time-capsule)
### The Bridge (new testament echo)
- Jesus with the Passover lamb at his feet — "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (John 1:29)  → closing bridge slide using jesus-a
