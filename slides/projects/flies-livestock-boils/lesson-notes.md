## Opening (after the 18-scene story)

> *What is Angela documenting today? **Sneaky Pharaoh.*** Pharaoh never gave God a flat "no" — he kept finding sneaky ways to look like he was obeying while never really letting God's people go. That is **incomplete obedience**, and it is still disobedience.

---

## Point 1 — "Sacrifice here in Egypt"

- **Pull picture:** `scene-1/1d` (Pharaoh's sly offer; Moses refusing)
- **Scripture:** Exodus 8:25–27
- **Teacher's Note:** Pharaoh told the Israelites to sacrifice to God *inside Egypt*. He knew this was a trap: the animals Israel would sacrifice were sacred to the Egyptians, so doing it openly could have provoked the Egyptians to stone them (Moses says exactly this in 8:26). It may have been a veiled attempt at mass killing — obedience on Pharaoh's terms, designed to destroy them.
- **Angela's diary line:** *"Pharaoh tried to avoid obedience by making the Israelites sacrifice in Egypt."*

## Point 2 — Delayed obedience ("tomorrow")

- **Pull picture:** `scene-1/1e` (Pharaoh stalling: "only not far... pray for me"). **Best single picture is in last week's deck** — `frogs-and-gnats scene-1/1f`/`1h` where Pharaoh chooses "tomorrow" for relief (Exo 8:9–10). Pull from there if available.
- **Scripture:** Exodus 8:9–10; 8:29
- **Teacher's Note:** Pharaoh could have ended the suffering immediately by obeying at once. Instead he kept choosing "tomorrow." Delay was his way of holding on to control.
- **Angela's diary line:** *"This is delayed obedience."*

## Point 3 — Pharaoh the liar

- **Pull picture:** `scene-1/1e` (Pharaoh's coaxing promise; Moses warning against deceit)
- **Scripture:** Exodus 8:28–29 (cf. John 8:44)
- **Teacher's Note:** Every time Pharaoh promised to let the people go, he was lying — Moses calls it out directly in 8:29 ("let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more"). Lying is the devil's nature (John 8:44); Pharaoh mirrors his master.
- **Angela's diary line:** *"Pharaoh was a liar, like his master, the devil."*

## Point 4 — Partial obedience has painful rewards

- **Pull picture:** `scene-1/1c` and `scene-2/2d` (the demarcation — Goshen safe and bright while Egypt suffers)
- **Scripture:** Exodus 8:22–23; 9:4–6
- **Teacher's Note:** God drew a clear line between his people and Egypt — protecting Goshen from flies and sparing Israel's livestock. The point for us: when we obey God only partway, we stay on the wrong side of that line and keep reaping painful consequences, plague after plague. Full obedience is the only safe place.
- **Angela's diary line:** *"Partial obedience has painful rewards."*

## Close

> Pharaoh's heart only grew harder the longer he played sneaky games with God (Exo 9:12). The safe, happy place is Goshen's side of the line — **obey God fully, and obey today.**
