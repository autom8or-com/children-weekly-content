# QUIZ SUNDAY TEMPLATE
## 3 MCQ Variants with Unified Answer Keys
### (Adaptable to ANY Bible Passage)

---

## OVERVIEW
- **Time:** 55 minutes total
- **Participants:** 20-30 teens (individual, open-book format)
- **Structure:** 3 separate question variants (A, B, C) + 1 unified answer key document
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice (MCQ) only
- **Total Questions:** 30 per variant
- **Scoring:** 1 point per question = 30 points total
- **Difficulty Distribution:** Each variant has equal distribution of easy/moderate/hard questions
- **Section Coverage:** Questions drawn from 5 narrative sections (not labeled in quiz)
- **Layout:** 2-column format per page (to conserve space)

---

## HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE

### Step 1: Identify Your 5 Narrative Sections
Break your Bible passage/chapter into **5 key narrative sections**:
- Section 1: [Opening event / Introduction]
- Section 2: [Development / Conflict]
- Section 3: [Crisis / Opposition]
- Section 4: [Turning point / Transformation]
- Section 5: [Resolution / Broader principle]

**Questions per section:** 6 questions per section × 5 sections = 30 questions total

### Step 2: Determine Difficulty Distribution
Decide your difficulty breakdown across ALL 30 questions:
- **Easy (Recall):** 8-10 questions (surface comprehension, factual recall)
- **Moderate (Comprehension/Application):** 12-14 questions (understanding cause-effect, application)
- **Hard (Analysis/Synthesis):** 6-8 questions (deeper reasoning, theological depth)

**Important:** Each of your 3 variants (A, B, C) must have the SAME difficulty distribution.
- Example: If Variant A has 8 Easy + 14 Moderate + 8 Hard, then Variant B and C also have 8 Easy + 14 Moderate + 8 Hard (just different questions)

### Step 3: Create Question Types
For variety within difficulty levels, use these MCQ types:

**RECALL Questions (Easy):**
- Factual: "What did [character] do in [section]?"
- Direct quote identification: "Who said, '[quote]'?"
- Sequence: "What happened first?"

**COMPREHENSION Questions (Moderate):**
- Cause-effect: "Why did [event] happen?"
- Character motivation: "What was [character's] reason for [action]?"
- Consequence: "What resulted from [action]?"

**APPLICATION Questions (Moderate-Hard):**
- Modern connection: "Which modern situation is most similar to [biblical event]?"
- Principle extraction: "What biblical principle does [event] demonstrate?"
- Conditional: "If [scenario], what should a Christian do based on [passage]?"

**ANALYSIS Questions (Hard):**
- Comparison: "How is [character A's action] different from [character B's action], and why?"
- Implication: "What does [event] reveal about God's character?"
- Synthesis: "How do [event 1] and [event 2] together teach us about [principle]?"

### Step 4: Create 3 Unique Variants
For each of the 30 questions:
1. Write a question for **Variant A**
2. Write a DIFFERENT question testing the same concept for **Variant B**
3. Write a DIFFERENT question testing the same concept for **Variant C**

**Key:** Different questions, same difficulty level, same concept being tested

**Example (all testing understanding of Section 1 event):**

**Variant A (Recall - Easy):**
"What did Paul do when he found the disciples of John the Baptist?
A) He rejected them
B) He baptized them in Jesus' name and laid hands on them
C) He taught them about John's baptism
D) He sent them away"

**Variant B (Recall - Easy, Different Question):**
"According to the passage, the disciples of John the Baptist had NOT yet received what?
A) Baptism
B) The Holy Spirit
C) Faith in God
D) Instruction from Paul"

**Variant C (Recall - Easy, Different Question):**
"When Paul asked the disciples about the Holy Spirit, what was their response?
A) They said they had received it
B) They asked what the Holy Spirit was
C) They said they had not even heard of it
D) They said they were waiting for it"

All three test the SAME concept (understanding the disciples' initial state) at the SAME difficulty level (Recall), but ask DIFFERENT questions.

### Step 5: Create Answer Keys
Create ONE document (Quiz_Answer_Keys.md) containing all 3 answer keys:

```
ANSWER KEY - VARIANT A
Question 1: B
Question 2: A
Question 3: C
... [etc for all 30]

ANSWER KEY - VARIANT B
Question 1: B
Question 2: C
Question 3: D
... [etc for all 30]

ANSWER KEY - VARIANT C
Question 1: C
Question 2: B
Question 3: A
... [etc for all 30]
```

### Step 6: Format for 2-Column Layout
When creating the actual question documents (Quiz_Variant_A.md, etc.):
- Set up 2 columns per page
- Each question with its 4 options fits in one column
- Footer appears below each question
- Saves paper and space

## DEBRIEF DISCUSSION (10 minutes)

After collecting quizzes, lead a brief discussion:

**Suggested Questions:**
1. "What was the most important moment or truth from [PASSAGE]?"
2. "How does [KEY PRINCIPLE] apply to your life as a teen?"
3. "What surprised you or stood out most about [PASSAGE]?"

**Closing Statement:**
Connect the quiz back to the overarching biblical truth of the passage. Keep it brief (2-3 minutes).

---

## PREVENTING CHEATING WITH 3 VARIANTS

**Seating arrangement matters!**
- Assign variants strategically based on seating
- Teens sitting next to each other get different variants
- Example: Row 1: A, B, C, A, B, C... pattern
- Or use a checkerboard: A in corners, B on sides, C in middle

**During Quiz:**
- Remind students: "You have different questions—focus on YOUR quiz only"
- Variants look different enough that copying is obvious
- Monitor seating to prevent sharing

## KEY PRINCIPLE

The goal of Quiz Sunday is to assess **comprehension and biblical understanding**, not penalize sitting arrangement. Using 3 variants with equal difficulty levels achieves both: **academic integrity** (each student gets unique questions) and **fairness** (all students graded on same 30-point scale).
